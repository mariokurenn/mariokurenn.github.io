"What's a good conversion rate?" is the most-asked question in CRO — and the most misleading one.

A 2% conversion rate is excellent for a luxury furniture store. It's a disaster for a SaaS free trial signup page. The only conversion rate that matters is yours, relative to your own baseline.

That said, benchmarks are useful. They show you what's possible, where you rank, and where the biggest improvement opportunities exist. This guide covers 2026 conversion rate benchmarks across the industries we work with most.

## How Conversion Rates Are Measured

Before the benchmarks, a critical note: conversion rates are only comparable when the denominator is the same.

**Sessions-based CVR**: Conversions ÷ Total Sessions  
**Users-based CVR**: Conversions ÷ Unique Users  
**Visitors-based CVR**: Conversions ÷ Total Visitors  

Platforms report differently. Shopify uses sessions. Google Analytics uses users. Different tools give different numbers for the same site.

When comparing your CVR to a benchmark, confirm you're measuring the same thing. Most industry benchmarks use sessions-based CVR.

### Ecommerce CVR by Device

| Device | Avg CVR |
|--------|---------|
| Desktop | 3.5–4.5% |
| Tablet | 2.5–3.5% |
| Mobile | 1.5–2.5% |

Mobile traffic typically represents 60%+ of sessions but converts at half the rate of desktop. This gap is the single biggest CVR opportunity for most ecommerce businesses. Closing the mobile-desktop gap from 2:1 to 1.5:1 can deliver 15–25% overall CVR improvements.

## SaaS Conversion Rate Benchmarks

SaaS CVR is more complex because there are multiple conversion points: website visitor → trial, trial → paid, and visitor → demo/lead.

### SaaS Free Trial Conversion Rates

| Type | Avg CVR | Top Performers |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Free trial (credit card required) | 25–40% of trials → paid | 50–60% |
| Free trial (no credit card) | 5–15% of trials → paid | 20–25% |
| Freemium → paid | 2–5% | 8–15% |

**Freemium vs. free trial**: Freemium models have lower trial-to-paid rates but can generate enormous volumes of users. Free trial with credit card has the highest trial-to-paid rate but lower trial volume. Choose based on your acquisition cost and product complexity.

### SaaS Website Visitor → Trial/Demo

| Page type | Avg CVR |
|-----------|---------|
| Pricing page → trial/demo | 8–15% |
| Homepage → any CTA | 2–5% |
| Blog post → email/trial | 0.5–2% |
| Case study → demo request | 5–12% |
| Feature page → trial | 3–8% |

### SaaS Lead Gen (Demo Requests)

| Company type | Demo request CVR |
|-------------|-----------------|
| SMB-focused SaaS | 2–5% of website visitors |
| Enterprise SaaS | 0.5–2% of visitors |

## Landing Page Conversion Rate Benchmarks

Landing pages — single-purpose pages designed for one conversion action — have higher CVRs than general website pages.

### By Page Type

| Page Type | Avg CVR | Top 25% |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Paid search landing pages | 4–8% | 12%+ |
| Lead gen landing pages | 5–15% | 25%+ |
| Webinar registration | 20–35% | 50%+ |
| Free tool / resource | 8–20% | 30%+ |
| Product demo page | 3–8% | 15%+ |
| Email capture / squeeze | 10–25% | 40%+ |

### Factors That Drive Landing Page CVR Variance

**High-converting landing pages share these traits:**
- Clear, benefit-focused headline
- One primary CTA (not multiple competing options)
- Social proof near the CTA
- Fast load time (under 2 seconds)
- Mobile-optimized form
- Strong message match with the traffic source

The gap between average (5%) and top performers (30%+) for the same page type is almost entirely explained by these factors.

## How to Use Benchmarks Effectively

Benchmarks are a compass, not a destination. Here's how to use them correctly:

### 1. Compare to your own baseline first

Before comparing to industry averages, establish your current baseline. A 2.5% CVR improving to 3.5% is a 40% improvement regardless of what competitors are doing. Your primary benchmark is your own historical performance.

### 2. Segment your comparison

Your overall CVR is less useful than your CVR by traffic source, device, and page type. An aggregate 2% CVR might hide a 0.8% mobile CVR — which is your real problem.

### 3. Identify the right comparable

A luxury fashion brand shouldn't compare their CVR to "average ecommerce." They should compare to other luxury fashion brands. Narrow your benchmark to the right category.

### 4. Focus on the gap between you and top performers

Average benchmarks tell you where you are. Top-performer benchmarks tell you what's possible. The difference is your opportunity. If top 10% performers in your category convert at 6% and you're at 2%, you have a 3x improvement opportunity.

### 5. Don't optimize to the benchmark

The goal isn't to reach "average." The goal is to reach your personal maximum — and then exceed what the data says is possible. Top performers got there by testing relentlessly, not by targeting the average.

## What a Good Conversion Rate Actually Looks Like for You

Here's a framework:

- **Below category average**: Significant structural issues. Start with a full [CRO audit](/services/cro-audit/) to identify critical blockers.
- **At category average**: Solid foundation. Focus on systematic A/B testing to move toward top-quartile performance.
- **Top 25% of your category**: Strong CRO program already. Focus on advanced personalization, segmentation, and lifecycle optimization.
- **Top 10%**: You're building a genuine competitive advantage. The next lever is typically revenue per visitor (AOV + CVR combined).

The best time to start improving your conversion rate is now. Every month at 2% when you could be at 3% is real revenue left behind.
